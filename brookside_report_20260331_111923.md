### 3. HIGH-LEVEL SUMMARY

#### **Key Insights**
*   **Plant-Based Pivot:** Brookside is successfully transitioning from a traditional dairy-only processor to a "nutrition company," with its Oat and Almond milk lines gaining significant traction among urban, health-conscious Kenyans.
*   **Farmer-Centric Branding:** The brand maintains a strong "social license" by consistently publicizing farmer bonuses and price increases, which acts as a buffer against political criticism.
*   **Trade Resilience:** Despite the "Uganda-Kenya milk war" and import blocks, the brand's pivot to North African markets (Algeria) is viewed by business analysts as a masterclass in "dairy diplomacy."

#### **Sample Sentiment Posts**
*   **Positive:** *"Finally, a local brand doing Oat milk right! Brookside is saving my lactose-intolerant mornings. #HealthyLivingKE"*
*   **Neutral:** *"Brookside increasing farmer prices is good, but let's see if the 500ml packet stays at the same price in the supermarket."*
*   **Negative:** *"Why is the Kenya Dairy Board still blocking Brookside Uganda? We want variety and fair prices, not trade wars. #MilkPolitics"*

#### **Strategic Takeaway for Marketing Teams**
**Double down on the "Farmer-First" narrative.** As drought conditions potentially drive up consumer prices in Q2 2026, Brookside should use its social platforms to show *why* the price is higher—highlighting the direct support given to 160,000+ Kenyan families. This transparency will mitigate "corporate greed" accusations during inflationary periods.
